# Pack composition findings — English 1999 Base Set Unlimited

**Research accessed:** 2026-10-30, Central Daylight Time; date obtained from the host with `date '+%Y-%m-%d %Z'`.

**Status: research only; no generator, provisional model, implementation parameters or approximation approved.** Consequential unknowns below must be presented to the user before any approximation is implemented. Source labels are local descriptive labels, not global citation-ledger numbers. The parent workstream will register URLs.

## Scope and evidence language

The target is the ordinary **English, shadowed, ©1999 Base Set Unlimited booster**, not First Edition, Shadowless, ©1999–2000/Base 2000, foreign-language product, starter/theme decks, or later reprints. This is an identification boundary, **not a claim that all target packs had one factory, one production run or one collation layout**. Collector sources use “Unlimited” inconsistently, sometimes including Shadowless or later copyright variants. In particular, the sheet archive lists multiple numbered Unlimited runs and distinguishes sheets for preconstructed decks from boosters. [PACK-ID; SHEETS; SCOPE]

Evidence categories used here:

- **Documented claim:** wording directly present in a retrieved document; distinguish contemporaneous primary material from retrospective secondary writing. A documented collector claim is not automatically a documented factory specification.
- **Observed claim:** someone's reported opening, or this researcher's visual reading of a photographed artifact. Provenance, sample size and printing identification matter.
- **Mathematical deduction:** a conditional consequence of explicitly stated inputs, not independent historical evidence.
- **Unknown:** not established by the material retrieved. Absence of a located document does not prove no such document exists.

No manufacturer production specification, English 1999 Unlimited insertion-rate bulletin or complete factory collation algorithm was located. The primary materials reached were a wrapper photograph and archived rulebook OCR; neither establishes exact factory odds. No opening video was independently sampled for this report.

## 1. Composition, replacement and physical order

### Composition versus catalog rarity

The pack composition statement in MASTERGUIDE is explicit: “Each booster pack contained 11 trading cards; one rare, three uncommon, five commons, and two energy cards.” SEQUENCE independently describes the same aggregate composition in an opening-order report. Their agreement supports the ordinary composition but does not authenticate every pack variant or establish an error frequency. The collector rarity guide also distinguishes H, R, U, C and Basic Energy insertion categories. [MASTERGUIDE; SEQUENCE; RARITY-GUIDE]

“Common” and “uncommon” do **not** mean Pokémon-only: the secondary card table lists Trainer rarities and lists Double Colorless as Uncommon. Thus a pack could contain more than two cards whose card type is Energy when an uncommon is Double Colorless; that is not by itself evidence of an abnormal Basic Energy allocation. This is a **conditional deduction from the composition claim and catalog classification**, not a newly observed pack. The exact card catalog is another workstream. [MASTERGUIDE]

The rare-or-holo position is one position in the ordinary eleven-card account. MASTERGUIDE states: “The rare had a 1 in 3 chance of being a holographic rare.” That supports the **replacement relationship** more strongly than it supports the exact numerical probability. A holo pack is not documented here as retaining a separate non-holo rare. [MASTERGUIDE]

### Physical sequence is a distinct question

PokemonRadar's SEQUENCE post says: “We are all familiar with the base set unlimited having 5 commons, 2 energies, the rare, and then the 3 uncommons from front to back.” Taking that convention literally gives positions 1–5 common, 6–7 Basic Energy, 8 rare/holo, and 9–11 uncommon. The position numbering is a **deduction**, not additional factory evidence. A final reveal of the rare would require reordering relative to this reported sequence. [SEQUENCE]

**Limitations:** this is a 2019 retrospective report, not contemporary machinery documentation. The author does not provide an audited ©1999 Unlimited sample or a diagram tying “front” to wrapper artwork, card faces and opening direction. Another thread contains a conflicting, explicitly hesitant recollection (“Been a long time since I opened…”), without a clearly established printing. It should not override the more specific report or be merged into an invented universal sequence. [ORDER-RECOLLECTION]

The same SEQUENCE author reports different sequences for “4th print UK” packs depending on whether the rare is holo. Those packs are **outside the target printing** and do not prove a ©1999 Unlimited exception. They do demonstrate why a generic “Base Set order” claim must not be transferred across variants. First Edition/Shadowless observations in the post are also excluded, not blended into this target.

## 2. Holo frequency: convention is not an exact factory statistic

The located guide states one in three, while the rarity researcher explicitly describes the goal as “best-estimate” rarities and combines sheets, miscuts and YouTube opening observations. Its table uses H 0.333 / R 0.667 and a chosen 216-pack sequence. The author says: “however I am going to be using a sequence length 216 packs”. That is a **modelling choice**, not discovery of a guaranteed case-level factory cycle. This project has not adopted that model. [MASTERGUIDE; RARITY-GUIDE; RARITY-TABLE]

MASTERGUIDE additionally asserts that boxes had 36 packs, 12 containing holos. **Mathematical deduction:** 12/36 = 1/3. This consistency does not independently prove either input, an exact rate, independent trials, a guaranteed twelve holos per box or every-third-pack placement. An average and a fixed quota are different hypotheses. Packaging artwork association and mapping between neighboring packs also remain unestablished.

The rarity guide itself warns about video evidence: “the sample size available is not nearly large enough for these older sets to say anything for certain.” Its Energy discussion uses two opening videos, not a complete factory sample. Repeated websites saying “1 in 3” do not create independent trials or independent documentary confirmation. [RARITY-GUIDE]

**Finding to present:** one in three is the supported collector convention/approximation; **the exact target-run factory rate and dependence structure are unknown**. No confidence interval is reported because this report has no audited, representative dataset. Nothing here establishes the probability for an arbitrarily selected surviving loose pack.

## 3. Duplicates and restrictions

### What is established and what is not

- The primary rulebook's restriction on copies is a **deck-construction rule**: “you can't have more than 4 of any one card other than basic Energy cards in your deck”. It is not a booster-pack duplication restriction. Applying it to generated packs would be a category error. [RULEBOOK-V2]
- The rarity guide describes English packs as generally sequentially collated and suggests some sheet reorganization may avoid duplicates. Its wording is tentative: “Reorganizing the sheets may have been in order to avoid getting duplicate cards in one pack”. That is an inferred manufacturing explanation, not a guarantee that every ©1999 Unlimited pack has distinct common/uncommon identities. [RARITY-GUIDE]
- Repeated identities on a sheet do not by themselves establish whether duplicates occur inside one pack. One needs their spacings **and** the actual cutting/stacking sequence, feeder assignment, start offsets and pack boundaries. This is a logical limitation, not an observed historical rule.
- No authoritative target-printing rule was located for whether the two Basic Energies can match, how often each ordered pair occurs, or whether common/uncommon identity duplicates are absent, rare errors, or permitted by ordinary collation. Negative search results are recorded in the evidence folder, not treated as proof of impossibility.
- “Same name”, “same catalog card” and “same evolutionary family” are different constraints. No evidence here authorizes a ban on evolution-family co-occurrence or a cross-pack uniqueness guarantee.

**User-facing decision:** do not choose with-replacement draws, sample-without-replacement rarity pools, deduplicate the whole pack, or force distinct Energy types before the uncertainty and changed behavior are explained. A successful toy sample would test code, not authenticate factory duplication rules.

## 4. Sheet weighting and collation

### Retrospective documentary claims

The rarity guide reports a 10×11 Unlimited holo sheet, 121-position common and uncommon sheets, and a partially obscured Unlimited Basic Energy sheet. It explicitly says its **rare sheet is documented for Base 2000 and only assumed to apply to Unlimited**. It also assumes missing Energy positions from Chinese/Base Set 2 examples. Therefore the evidence strength is not identical for every column of its table. [RARITY-GUIDE]

SHEETS provides separate captions for “Unlimited Base Holo”, “Unlimited Base PCD Holo” and “Unlimited Base Starter Holo”, plus dated non-holo sheet descriptions. That is important because a sheet containing deck-exclusive cards is not automatically a booster feed sheet. Its numerical run labels should not be confused with the four broad collector design/copyright categories. [SHEETS; SCOPE]

A remotely viewed holo-sheet photograph shows a ten-column, eleven-row grid with repeated identities, consistent with the guide's 110-position description. No complete identity-by-identity count audit or readable run-date authentication was performed in this study. This is limited artifact corroboration, not a full factory reconstruction. [SHEET-HOLO]

### Collector table values — recorded as claims, NOT accepted parameters

The table headed “4) Unlimited” reports the following multiplicities. These are a transcription of the researcher's candidate sheet model, **not a generated distribution or recommendation to use these weights**. [RARITY-TABLE]

| Category | Claimed identities and positions | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Booster holos | 5 identities × 8 copies; 10 × 7; 110 total positions | Fifteen booster identities, not all sixteen set holos. Photographed layout does not establish all target-run layouts or insertion losses. |
| Non-holo rares | 9 × 8; 7 × 7; 121 positions | English target-run attribution inferred from a later ©1999–2000 sheet, not directly established. |
| Uncommons | 25 × 4; 7 × 3; 121 positions | Sheet counts do not establish exact per-pack sequence or deduplication policy. |
| Commons | 25 × 4; 7 × 3; 121 positions | Same limitation; distribution over production layouts is unestablished. |
| Basic Energy | Lightning 35; Psychic 34; Grass 17; Water 17; Fighting 9; Fire 9; 121 positions | Partially obscured sheet and cross-printing inference; the two 9-copy identities are inferred by omission in the table. Energy pair correlations are not supplied. |

The table names Chansey, Magneton, Nidoking, Poliwrath and Zapdos as its H8 group; the other ten booster holos are H7 by omission. Its uncommon/common exceptions are transcribed in the text-only viewing notes rather than converted into a project catalog. Source spellings are preserved there. [RARITY-TABLE]

**Conditional mathematics, not historical measurement:** if every usable position on a particular sheet reaches packs equally, an identity's long-run share among cards from that feed is `copies of that identity / usable sheet positions`. Multiplying by an insertion rate introduces an additional assumption. The guide's formula is “sheet size / (pack insertion rate * magnitude)” for its reciprocal pack rate. A sheet photograph alone cannot supply the holo insertion probability. [RARITY-GUIDE]

The multiplicity totals were checked with Python: holo 110; rare 121; common and uncommon 121 each; Basic Energy 121. These checks establish internal arithmetic consistency only, not authenticity or completeness of the history.

### What cannot be reconstructed from those totals

The exact stack traversal direction, alternative stacks, cutting order, feeder alternation, starting offsets, splices, wastage, sheet-run mixture and pack-boundary mapping are not established. Uniformly choosing cards with sheet-proportional marginal weights would still discard the **joint distribution** created by collation. Randomly selecting unique cards would impose a different joint distribution again.

The Collation Project is useful background on machinery concepts but is explicitly a **Magic: The Gathering** project, not direct evidence for this Pokémon printing. Similar publisher or factory names do not license transferring its algorithm. [COLLATION-BACKGROUND]

## 5. Decisions that must be brought to the user

No default is chosen here. Before implementing any approximation, present:

1. **Printing identity:** confirm ©1999 English shadowed Unlimited only; do not conflate later Base 2000/UK/Australian sequences or broad collector “print-run” numbering with this scope.
2. **Physical order fidelity:** explain the customary reported sequence and its orientation/sub-run uncertainty; distinguish historical stack order from a deliberate reveal presentation.
3. **Holo odds and independence:** explain that approximate one-in-three, exact independent 1/3, fixed quotas and deterministic cycles are different behaviors, only the first being supported here as collector convention.
4. **Duplication behavior:** explicitly discuss common/uncommon identity repeats and matching Basic Energy pairs; neither a blanket ban nor unrestricted repeats is verified.
5. **Within-rarity weights:** disclose the unequal-sheet evidence and the assumptions behind the rare/Energy columns; equal weighting is not neutral historical fidelity.
6. **Collation fidelity:** explain that matching marginal weights is not reconstructing factory pack combinations; no box/case simulator is in scope.
7. **Errors and variants:** no measured error-pack rate was established. Do not invent error odds or silently include excluded printing/date variants.

Further evidence that would materially reduce uncertainty: contemporary WotC product/odds documentation; photographed unfolded ©1999 Unlimited wrappers; independently authenticated target-run rare and Energy sheets; an opening dataset retaining original physical order and pack/printing identification; and factory feed/cutting documentation. An audit should distinguish independent artifacts from websites repeating the same source. This is a research backlog, not permission to implement.

## Retrieval and verification limitations

Google returned a redirect/interstitial rather than search results; Bing returned irrelevant “base” results. DuckDuckGo and directly retrieved collector pages provided the usable discovery path. The Smithsonian rulebook catalog URL `https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1807520` returned HTTP 403 verification, so its search excerpt was **not** promoted to read primary evidence; the Internet Archive text route provided the rulebook instead. Several targeted duplicate/order searches returned no results, which does not settle those questions.

A local text-only HTTP retrieval helper and evidence/link metadata are retained under `evidence/pack/`. Arithmetic and evidence-text matching were checked locally. Such checks establish document consistency only. They are not playable testing, a pack simulation, factory statistical validation, or a substitute for presenting unresolved choices to the user.
