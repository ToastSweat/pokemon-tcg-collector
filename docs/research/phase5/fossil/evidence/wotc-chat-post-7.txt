Featuring Master_Trainers Mike and Pat from Customer Service Master Trainer Pat: Hi Gang! We’ve done some homework for you guys with respect to hunting down some answers for last week. MT Mike Do you have those ready? Master Trainer Mike: Hi gang. Let’s start with some rules answers to last week’s questions. First Misty’s Staryu’s SWIFT attack. The SWIFT attack ignores all Pokémon Powers, Weaknesses, Resistances, or ANY other effects that attempt to alter the amount of damage done by this attack. SWIFT does NOT prevent anything that prevents the attack from actually occurring however like Smokescreen or Transparency. So SWIFT will ignore a Defender that is attached to the Defending Pokémon, as it is an affect that is attempting to alter the amount of damage done by the attack. Second, the RECALL Trainer cards. This has the same sort of effect as Metronome or Ditto in that any self-referencing in the attack is changed to the active Pokémon’s name instead. Meaning if you used RECALL to use the Energy Loop attack on Sabrina’s Abra when it is evolved to Sabrina’s Kadabra (or Sabrina’s Alakazam), you would replace the topmost evolution step’s name into the attack. In other words you would still have to return a psychic energy card attached to it in order to the Energy Loop attack. The easiest way to think of it is that the RECALL Trainer gives the Active Pokémon the attack of your choice from one of its previous evolutions, with all of its positive and negative effects affecting your active Pokémon. So if you used RECALL on Gyarados to use Magickarp’s Flail attack, it is possible for that attack to do up to 90 damage. Rocket’s Scyther’s Shadow Images attack is NOT cumulative. Clarification on the whole Knock Out/triggered effects steps in Pokémon as well. When a Pokémon receives sufficient damage to be knocked out it is INSTANTLY knocked out (before anything else is done). HOWEVER, Prize cards are NOT drawn AND replacement Pokémon is NOT moved up until ALL triggered effects are resolved. Meaning that if Blaine’s Moltres does its attack and Knocks out Dark Gyarados, the Dark Gyarados would get its chance with its Final Beam attack effect BEFORE the Moltres can return to your hand (if you flipped tails). Blaine’s Moltres states that it would be returned to your hand AFTER DOING DAMAGE. This means AFTER all triggered effects are resolved (in this case the Dark Gyarados’s Final Beam). So after everything has resolved THEN prizes are drawn and replacement Benched Pokémon are moved up (with the player whose turn is next choosing first). Aug 25, 2000 - Gym Heroes TCG Hour TCG Hour: Pokémon: Gym Heroes Featuring Robert Gutschera Fri., Aug. 25, 2000 WizO_Bilbo: Evenin’ folks and welcome to TCG Hour with our special guest, Robert Gutschera! From Cerulean City to Pewter City and beyond, you can find powerful Pokémon trainers called Gym Leaders. These are some of the best trainers around, and the Gym Heroes™ set lets you check out some of the gyms as well as the Gym Leaders’ personal Pokémon and training methods. Robert Gutschera from Wizards R&D gives you the scoop. WizO_Bilbo: Robert, do you have any comments to open up the chat with? WotC_Robert: I don’t have anything special to say . . . I’m happy to dive right in! guest22: Are these the same cards that are in the Japanese set? WotC_Robert: That’s a good (and surprisingly complicated!) question. Let me try to explain . . . . The cards are all the same cards as they appeared in Japan, but the way we are releasing them is different. They had a release of two decks; then two more decks; then boosters; then two more decks; then boosters again. There was a lot of time between these different releases, and it was very hard for fans to keep track of what came from where. We decided to simplify it so that there would be two releases: Gym Heroes and Gym Challenge. We also wanted people to be able to collect all the cards from the boosters. So if you want to buy a theme deck, that’s great, but if you just want to buy boosters you can do that too. In the Japanese releases, some cards were only available in theme decks. I hope that all made sense! guest22: That’s very interesting. Thanks! Scrye_Fung: Ancient Mew is the nickname for a card just like the movie promo Mewtwo is a nickname for a card. Why is this card considered different from Mew and you’re allowed to have four of each (Ancient Mew and Mew) in a deck? After all, there are only 3 symbols on top of the Ancient Mew card, which should indicate the word Mew. Also, now that Ancient Mew is banned from tournament play, why is it still being allowed at the DCI-sanctioned East Coast STS? WotC_Robert: Ancient Mew was never meant to be a tournament playable card . . . it was just meant to be for fun. We don’t want to have cards in play that people can’t read! And of course it has a different back. Remember we want to keep the environment fun and casual . . . don’t just think about the big tournaments with the top-level players (who of course are heavily represented in places like this chatroom!) In more casual environments, it’s important that people can read the cards, play them as written, play without sleeves if they want, and so on. Also, as far as I know, they don’t allow it in Japan, and we want to keep the same cards legal or not legal in the two places guest17: I participated in the Gym Heroes Prerelease tourney. I was charged an entry fee of $5 and had to pay extra for booster packs. They also allowed Japanese cards . . why did this happen? WotC_Robert: I’m sorry to hear it didn’t work out for you . . . it’s hard for me to respond to the specifics since I don’t know what that tourney organizer was doing, or what he had promised beforehand, and so on. But I would say the best thing to do is to write Wizards Customer Service in an email giving the particulars, and if the tourney organizer is playing fast and loose they can try to whip him into shape! King_Blastoise: Why did Wizards take some of the cards out of the original Gym set and throw in some Blaine and Sabrina cards? Why didn’t Wizards just keep Gym like the original one was set up? WotC_Robert: Good question. I’m the one responsible for all that (I picked the Heroes and Challenge sets out of the Japanese cards). The main reason was that we thought it was important to represent all the colors. We didn’t want some kid whose favorite color was Psychic to find that there were no cards at all for him in Heroes. We didn’t particularly want to make our set different just for the sake of it being different, though. Within the bounds of having all the colors, we tried to have the emphasis on Heroes be on the first four Gym Leaders and the emphasis in Challenge be on the second four. Remember, not everyone (especially younger kids) knows exactly what the Japanese release structure was. We wanted something that would make sense to someone just buying Heroes straight up without advance information about the Japanese sets. Sorry if it didn’t match what you wanted . . . I’m hoping that having all the different colors will appeal to lots of the people buying Heroes! guest22: Why is the trainer called Misty (which is a foil) rarer than most of the Pokémon? WotC_Robert: She shouldn’t be . . . I know it seems that some of the foils are rarer than others, but really they are all about the same! It might seem like one or another is rarer, but that’s mostly randomness having to do with time and place. There can be very slight differences . . . for example, if most foils go on the sheet four times, but there are still a few extra slots on the sheet, we put a few foils on five times rather than waste paper. But none of the foils are deliberately put on many times less often. We try to make them all as equally close in rarity as we can. Really. I’ve done a lot of the layouts (I did all the early ones, although I didn’t do the Heroes ones). Rulings from Creatures . . . it varies quite a bit. Typically what happens is that we save up our questions, and then the next time we see them (maybe to talk about an upcoming set, maybe at a big tournament or other event) we ask the questions then. Most of the questions never see the light of day . . . we ask lots of questions when we are writing the English versions of the cards, so that we can clear up as much as possible in terms of how the cards work *before* we actually print them . . . that way the English wordings can be clearer (at least that’s the goal!). Of course, if something really important comes up, we can try to hasten the process, but that’s how it usually works. The complexity of the question is usually less important than when we are scheduled to see them . . . it’s so far from here to Japan (and they are very good at giving us lots of time we do get together) that we usually have big meetings with lots of questions rather than lots of short questions. Imakuni of course being an example. I personally like the card, but specifically which cards we put into sets isn’t my decision anymore! So the full Japanese Gym set is much larger than it would seem from just the count in the two booster sets they did make. WotC_Robert: The sad answer is I don’t know exactly . . . I never asked the Creatures people this question. Erika and Blaine aren’t as well known from the show as Misty, so what Pokémon they have is more open. That’s just a guess though. guest22: Why do the same Pokémon have different designs and attacks? (for example, Misty’s Tentacool) WotC_Robert: The basic answer is that there are only 150 Pokémon, so if people still want new Pokémon cards, some Pokémon need to be repeated. Normally, of course, Pokémon with the same name but different powers (which you can think of as different levels of the same Pokémon, if you like) only appear in different sets. I’m not exactly sure why they decided to change that in Gym . . . my guess is that they wanted to have lots of cards that went well with a given Gym Leader. For example, Misty is very strongly associated with Staryu, so rather than doing lots of totally different Pokémon as “Misty’s Pokémon” (and have some of them not seem very “Misty-like”), they decided to do different versions of Staryu. Also, keep in mind that it would be really hard for the colors like Psychic and Lightning where there are very few Pokémon! WotC_Robert: Thank you all for coming! It was good to talk with all of you!
